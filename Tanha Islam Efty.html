Research

The Role of Filaggrin in the Stratum Corneum Barrier and Atopic Dermatitis

Undergraduate literature review, Department of Genetic Engineering and Biotechnology, University of Dhaka, 2026

Atopic dermatitis is the most common inflammatory skin disease in the world. Loss-of-function mutations in the filaggrin gene (FLG) are its strongest known genetic risk factor, raising risk roughly three- to five-fold. My review traces how filaggrin deficiency, whether inherited or caused by inflammatory cytokines, weakens the skin barrier, lets allergens and Staphylococcus aureus in, and drives inflammation that suppresses filaggrin further.

The review also follows filaggrin's role in the atopic march toward asthma, allergic rhinitis and food allergy. It highlights a clear gap: most FLG genetics comes from people of European ancestry, while South Asian populations carry a heavy disease burden and distinct variants. Closing that gap through population-specific sequencing and early-life biomarkers is the kind of work I hope to do.

  • Filaggrin

The cycle the review describes

  1. Filaggrin is lost through an FLG mutation or cytokine suppression
  2. The barrier leaks: more water loss, higher skin pH, altered lipids
  3. Allergens and S. aureus get through the skin
  4. Keratinocytes release alarmins, which drive TH2 inflammation
  5. IL-4 and IL-13 suppress filaggrin again, so the cycle repeats
